Choosing the right kitchen material

The right choice comes down to three things: how it looks, how much upkeep you want, and your budget. Here's how the popular options actually behave behind a working stove.

Glass tileReflects light and adds depth — great for brightening darker kitchens. Non-porous, so it resists stains and wipes clean. Our specialty: custom blends.
Porcelain & ceramicThe low-maintenance workhorse. Durable, water-resistant, and the most budget-friendly, from classic subway to large panels.
Natural stoneMarble, quartzite, and travertine bring unmatched character. Porous, so it wants sealing and a little more care — worth it for a statement.
Large-formatFewer grout lines mean a cleaner, more modern look and less to scrub. Ideal for slab backsplashes that run counter-to-cabinet.

Not sure which way to go? Bring photos or samples of your counters and cabinets and we'll pull coordinating options. Matching undertones and finish — warm vs. cool, matte vs. polished — is the step that separates a backsplash that looks designed from one that looks close-but-off.

Kitchen tile FAQ

What tile is best for a kitchen backsplash?
For most kitchens, glazed ceramic, porcelain, and glass tile are the easiest to live with: they resist stains and moisture and wipe clean behind a cooktop. Glass tile blends add depth and reflect light, which suits darker LA kitchens. Natural stone — marble, quartzite, travertine — is beautiful but porous, so it should be sealed and is best kept away from constant grease if you want it low-maintenance. We help you weigh look, upkeep, and budget for your specific kitchen.
Do you supply the tile and deliver it to my job site?
Yes. We are a supplier, not just a showroom: we quote wholesale and builder pricing and deliver free to your job site anywhere in Greater Los Angeles, so you or your installer aren't hauling boxes across the city.
How much tile should I order for my kitchen?
Measure the backsplash area in square feet, then add roughly 10–15% for cuts, breakage, and future repairs (more for mosaics, diagonal layouts, or patterned tile). Send us your measurements or a rough sketch and we'll confirm quantities so you order once and don't run short mid-job.
Can you match tile to my countertops and cabinets?
Yes. Bring photos or samples of your counters, cabinets, and flooring and we'll pull tile and stone options that coordinate in tone, finish, and scale. Matching undertones is the step most DIY backsplashes get wrong, so it's worth doing before you order.
