Stone rewards a little knowledge. The stones, at a glance

MarbleThe classic. Unmatched veining and luxury; softer and acid-sensitive, so it's happiest on walls, vanities, and lower-traffic surfaces.
QuartziteNatural quartzite gives marble's look with more hardness and stain resistance — a favorite for kitchens.
TravertineWarm, earthy, and timeless; beautiful honed or tumbled for floors, showers, and outdoor spaces.
LimestoneSoft, even tones for a calm, natural look on walls and floors.

Two things to plan for: stone should be sealed on install and resealed over time, and finish matters — honed for grip and wear on floors and wet areas, polished to show off color where it's protected. Slab or tile — and caring for stone

Natural stone comes two ways, and the right one depends on the job. Slabs give you large, continuous surfaces with book-matched veining for counters, vanities, and dramatic feature walls; stone tile is easier to handle and set for floors, showers, and backsplashes. Either way, care is simple but real: seal on installation, wipe spills promptly — especially acidic ones like wine, citrus, and coffee on marble — use pH-neutral stone cleaners rather than generic acidic ones, and reseal periodically, more often in kitchens and wet areas. Done routinely, that keeps stone resisting stains for decades. We'll tell you exactly what a given stone needs before you fall for it, so there are no surprises later.

Natural stone FAQ

What types of natural stone do you supply?
Marble, quartzite, travertine, and limestone are the mainstays, in both tile and slab form, along with stacked/ledger stone for walls. Each has its own personality: marble for classic luxury, quartzite for marble looks with more durability, travertine and limestone for warm, earthy tones.
Does natural stone need sealing?
Yes. Stone is porous, so it should be sealed on installation and resealed periodically — more often in wet or high-use areas. Sealing is simple and it's what keeps stone resisting stains and moisture. We'll tell you what a given stone needs before you commit to it.
What's the difference between honed and polished stone?
Polished stone is glossy and shows off color and veining, but it also shows etching and water spots more and can be slick underfoot. Honed stone has a soft matte finish that hides wear and offers more grip — usually the better call for floors and wet areas. Same stone, different finish and feel.
Is quartzite better than marble?
It depends on the use. Marble is the classic, but it's softer and etches from acids (lemon, wine). Natural quartzite gives a similar look with more hardness and stain resistance, which many clients prefer for kitchens. We'll walk you through the trade-offs for your project.
