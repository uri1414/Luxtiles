## 1. The LA tile & stone landscape

The Greater Los Angeles tile/stone market has four competitor tiers. Lux competes in
a different lane than most: **wholesale/builder pricing + free job-site delivery +
commercial capability**, versus retail-showroom or pure-import models.

| Tier | Who | Typical strength | Where they're weak (our opening) |
|------|-----|------------------|----------------------------------|
| **National distributors w/ LA showrooms** | Bedrosians, Arizona Tile, Daltile, MSI | Huge catalog, strong domains, many locations | Impersonal; not positioned on delivery + builder relationships; weak on hyper-local city intent |
| **Regional / boutique showrooms** | Mission Tile West, Mission Stone, local design showrooms | Design cachet, curated ranges, good photography | Premium retail pricing; thin on commercial + wholesale messaging |
| **Wholesale / import yards** | Independent importers, stone yards | Price, inventory depth | Poor/thin websites, little SEO, weak schema, no content |
| **Installers / contractors** | Tile setters, GCs | Rank for "tile installer {city}" | Sell labor, not supply; don't own "tile store {city}" supply intent well |

**Strategic read:** the national chains own broad head terms and have domain strength
we won't beat quickly. The realistic wins are **(a) long-tail application + material +
city intent** ("glass tile backsplash Calabasas", "pool waterline tile Encino"),
**(b) commercial-supplier intent** where big-box retail is weakly positioned, and
**(c) speed/UX/schema quality** where import yards and installers under-invest.

## 3. What the leaders do that we should match

- **Product/collection depth** with real imagery and consistent schema.
- **Location pages** (the chains have many) — but generic. We can win city intent with
  *genuinely local* pages (nearby projects, neighborhoods, delivery notes).
- **Fast, mobile-first** experiences with clean Core Web Vitals.
- **Review/GBP presence** — the chains have volume; we start at zero and must build
  **real** reviews (never fabricated) via a post-delivery review flow.

## 4. Keyword gaps & opportunities (prioritized)

1. **Application + city long-tail** — "kitchen backsplash tile Calabasas", "bathroom
   tile Encino", "pool tile Pasadena". Lower volume, low difficulty, high intent, and
   under-served by the national chains. **Best early ROI.**
2. **Commercial supplier intent** — "commercial tile supplier Los Angeles", "large
   format tile for hotels/apartments", "builder tile pricing LA". Retail showrooms
   barely address this; our commercial division is a differentiator.
3. **Material education** — "glass tile vs porcelain", "large format tile pros and
   cons", "natural stone for showers". Feeds AI Overviews / ChatGPT / Perplexity
   citations and builds topical authority.
4. **"Free delivery / wholesale" modifiers** — a genuine, ownable differentiator few
   competitors lead with.

## 5. E-E-A-T posture vs competitors

We can't out-authority national brands on day one, so we win trust on **Experience**
and **local specificity**:

- **Experience:** real project photos with real LA locations (the portfolio is the
  single most valuable E-E-A-T asset — prioritize collecting it), "1,000+ projects
  supplied", named point of contact (Danny).
- **Expertise:** material guides written from supplier knowledge (blends, large-format
  handling, waterline tile).
- **Trust:** Licensed & Insured, consistent NAP everywhere, real reviews only,
  transparent service area and contact. **Do not** publish invented ratings,
  testimonials, credentials, or address — Google + FTC violations and an E-E-A-T risk.
